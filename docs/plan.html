Project plan · 5 Oct 2026 · rev 12

Glugg

Glugg, Swedish for a small opening in a wall, keeps watch over the windows of your wood workshop. An ESP32-S3 pairs with IKEA MYGGBETT window sensors and lights one LED per open window on an 8-LED strip. Your ESP32-H2 is its Thread radio. Sensor 1 gets LED 1, sensor 2 gets LED 2, in the order they are added. The workshop has four rooms with about 30 windows each, so the finished system is one Glugg per room, each showing its own windows.

Mean Well RS-15-5 (or HDR-15-5) Waveshare ESP32-S3-Zero-N8R8 (new) Waveshare ESP32-H2-Zero 8× WS2812 stick (4–7 VDC) IKEA MYGGBETT E2492

Where this stands

Progress

The toolchain is installed. The offline lookup, the plan's go/no-go question, passed in simulation. The full S3 firmware (pairing, subscriptions, LEDs and console) is written and builds, and its app logic passes its tests on the build machine. A review against the Matter, OpenThread and ESP-IDF sources then fixed several bugs that would have shown up on the first pairing, and added a startup animation. Nothing has run on the boards yet: the parts order went in on 30 Sep. One 1 kΩ resistor was added to the parts list afterwards.

Design

The ESP32-S3-Zero-N8R8 (8 MB flash, 8 MB PSRAM) runs the Matter controller: it pairs the sensors, keeps track of them and drives the LEDs. The ESP32-H2-Zero is reflashed as its Thread radio, and the two boards talk over two UART wires. Espressif tests exactly this pairing: an S3 with PSRAM running the controller plus a Thread border router, with an H2 as the radio.

Sensor

MYGGBETT uses Matter over Thread only; nobody has found a way to pair it over Zigbee. It pairs over Bluetooth, so the S3 handles that, and after pairing it talks over Thread through the H2.

Network

Fully offline, by your choice: no Wi-Fi and no network credentials on the device. The S3 therefore runs the Thread network's lookup service (SRP and DNS-SD) itself. Espressif's example doesn't do this, but an OpenThread simulation shows it works, and Matter points its lookups at the S3's own service without extra code.

Scale

Four rooms, about 30 windows each. Decided 1 Oct: one Glugg unit per room, each with its own S3, H2, Thread network and LED strip (one LED per window), instead of one central controller for all 120 sensors. Each unit then handles about 30 sensors, a modest step up from 8, and the rooms fail independently. The first unit is built and proven with 8 sensors first (Phases 2–4); scaling is Phase 6.

Main risks

First, two open Espressif bug reports that show other IKEA devices from the same product line failing to pair over Bluetooth. The likely cause is now found and fixed in the firmware build, but only a real pairing can confirm it. Second, two parts of the offline lookup that only the real S3 can prove. Both are the first tests once the boards arrive.

1 · Wiring

Wiring diagram

One 5 V supply feeds both boards and the LED stick through a shared +5 V and GND rail. Each board gets its own Schottky diode, because both boards wire their 5V pin straight to their USB port. The diode keeps a computer's USB port from feeding the rail, but the rail's 5 V still reaches each board's USB-C socket. So connect the S3 to your computer through a USB hub or adapter that stays plugged into the computer (see Safety below). The S3's 3.3 V data line goes through a 5 V buffer to reach the LEDs. The H2 connects to the S3 with two crossed UART wires: each board's TX goes to the other board's RX, and the S3's TX wire has a 1 kΩ resistor at the S3 end.

Not in the list

  • The MYGGBETT sensors, from IKEA
  • Fused switch or breaker, mains lead, and an enclosure for the supply. These depend on how you install it.
  • Pin headers: both boards come without them
  • Hook-up wire, terminal blocks for the rails, and something to build on, such as perfboard, plus an optional DIP-14 socket for the 74HCT125

Alternatives

  • No mains wiring: a plug-in 5 V 2.4 A adapter (41019070, 79.20) and a panel DC jack (41015531, 22.40) replace the HDR-15-5. The jack's centre pin goes to the +5 V rail and its sleeve to GND. This is the simplest and safest option if the supply doesn't need to be built in.
  • Not recommended: the Mean Well EPS-15-5 (41037509, 78.48) is an open-frame board with bare mains connections, and only 3 were in stock.
  • Cheaper LEDs: a generic 8-LED WS2812 stick (41016857, 31.20) is wired the same way (5 V, GND, data in). It isn't branded, and its pad labels may differ from the diagram.
  • Any of these supplies is enough: the whole build draws about 1 A at most.

Safety and order of work

  • The RS-15-5 is built to go inside an enclosure: its mains terminals are exposed. The HDR-15-5's terminals are covered, but the same rule applies to both: keep the supply unplugged whenever you wire it. If you aren't comfortable wiring mains, have an electrician connect the mains side.
  • Connect GND first, then +5 V, then the signal wires. Disconnect in reverse order.
  • With the supply on, each board's USB-C socket already has about 4.7 V on it, and a computer's USB-C port may refuse to connect to a socket that is already powered. Connect the S3 through something that stays plugged into the computer, such as a USB hub, a dock, or a USB-C to USB-A adapter with an A-to-C cable, and plug and unplug only at the board end. Then the order doesn't matter. With a direct C-to-C cable, plug it in before switching the supply on.
  • Each diode drops about 0.3 V. The ~4.7 V left is fine for both boards, so leave the supply at 5.0 V.
2 · Architecture

How the boards talk to the sensors

MYGGBETT pairs over Bluetooth, then lives on a Thread network as a battery device that sleeps between check-ins. The S3 does the thinking: it pairs sensors over its own Bluetooth radio, runs the Thread network and its lookup service, and stays subscribed to each sensor's open/closed state. The H2 only carries Thread traffic, using the RCP (radio co-processor) firmware Espressif provides. The two boards have separate radios, so pairing a new sensor doesn't interrupt the ones already paired.

What happens when you pair a sensor

  1. You put the sensor in pairing mode (it opens a 15-minute window when you insert the battery or press its button once). Then you type pair 3497-011-2332 in the S3's serial console, using the 11-digit code printed on the sensor. The S3 checks the code's check digit, reserves the lowest free slot, and that LED starts pulsing green.
  2. The S3 finds the sensor over Bluetooth and opens a secure session using that code.
  3. The S3 checks the sensor's certificate against IKEA's root certificate (IKEA of Sweden Matter PAA G1), which is stored on the S3.
  4. The S3 hands over the Thread network credentials. The sensor joins the network through the H2 radio and registers its address with the S3's lookup service.
  5. The S3 looks the sensor up on Thread through its own lookup service and completes pairing. The slot is saved to flash.
  6. The S3 subscribes to the sensor's open/closed state. Every report updates that slot's LED.

If any step fails, or pairing hasn't finished after 5 minutes, the slot is freed again.

LED behaviour

Confirmed. The exact values below are what the firmware now uses. The last three states were added on 30 Sep.

Open
Warm amber at about 25% brightness (red 64, green 25 of 255)
Closed or empty slot
Off
Pairing into this slot
Slow green pulse, one breath every 3 s, until pairing succeeds or fails
Sensor not heard from
Dim blue blink, 0.3 s every 2 s, after 2 minutes without a working connection
Starting up
A dim white dot sweeps across all 8 LEDs, 1.4 s there and back, until the Thread network and the lookup service are up. Expect about 10–20 s after power-on.
Waiting for the first report
Steady dim white on a paired sensor that hasn't reported since boot or since pairing. Usually gone within a few seconds of the network coming up; blue after 2 minutes.
Thread radio not answering
All LEDs red for 3 s, then the S3 restarts. Check the UART wires and the H2's firmware.

Removing a sensor frees its slot, and the next sensor you pair takes the lowest free slot. The other sensors keep their LEDs. At most 8 sensors on the first unit, one per LED; room units will take about 30 (Phase 6). Slots are numbered 1–8 on the console, and LED 1 is the end nearest DIN. When a sleepy sensor's connection drops and comes back as part of normal operation, it gets a new 2-minute grace period, so routine reconnects don't flash blue.

4 · Work plan

Phases and checkpoints

Each phase ends with a checkpoint to pass before the next one starts. Toolchain: ESP-IDF v5.5.5 and esp-matter release/v1.6 (the latest stable pair), plus espressif/led_strip 3.0.3. The S3 firmware starts from esp-matter's controller example with its border-router config (sdkconfig.defaults.otbr). The H2 runs ESP-IDF's ot_rcp example.

Toolchain and hardware check

Install ESP-IDF v5.5.5 and esp-matter v1.6. Confirm the S3's flash and PSRAM with esptool.py flash_id. Flash ot_rcp to the H2 over its USB, configured to talk over the TX/RX pins (UART0) at 460 800 baud, the default on both sides. Wire everything as above. Flash a small test program to the S3 that steps through all 8 LEDs on GPIO13 with a brightness cap.

Partly doneESP-IDF v5.5.5 and esp-matter v1.6 are installed on the build machine. esp-matter's installer needed a one-line fix for Python 3.13. The LED test firmware is built for both boards; until the S3 arrives it can run on the H2 with LED data on GPIO4.

CheckpointAll 8 LEDs show the right colours on supply power, with USB plugged in at the same time, and without flicker. The console attaches through a hub whichever of supply and cable goes first. The S3 reports 8 MB of PSRAM.

1

Offline Thread network and lookup service

Build the controller example with the border-router config, but with Wi-Fi turned off. Point its radio UART at GPIO5/GPIO6, and set the PSRAM mode from Phase 0. Create the Thread network from firmware. Turn the OpenThread SRP and DNS clients back on and start the SRP and DNS-SD servers by hand. Lookups then go to the S3's own address automatically.

Passed in simulationTwo simulated OpenThread nodes proved the lookup, including a controller reboot. The offline controller firmware builds. The on-device checkpoint below is still open.

CheckpointThe S3 talks to the H2 and becomes Thread leader. The lookup service is running, and the S3 can register a test service with it and resolve that service again. The network survives a reboot.

2

Pair one MYGGBETT

Load IKEA's root certificate. Pair one sensor from the console with pair <code>, then check that its subscription to StateValue on endpoint 1 comes up.

Waiting for the S3The firmware for this step is written and builds, with IKEA's root certificate bundled and the Bluetooth pairing fix applied.

CheckpointPairing succeeds and opening and closing the window shows up within a few seconds. If pairing fails with error: ac, stop and debug that before going any further.

3

The application

  • Slot manager: slot n ↔ node ID in flash, filling the lowest free slot, 8 slots maximum
  • Subscription manager: subscribe to StateValue for each sensor, resubscribe after boot and after drops with backoff, and mark a sensor as not heard from
  • LED renderer: turns slot states into colours, with the brightness cap and the pairing animation
  • Console commands: pair <code>, list, remove <slot>, identify <slot>, factory-reset confirm

Written, not runAll four parts are written and build. The logic that doesn't need Matter (slots, backoff, LED states, pairing codes, console parsing, channel choice) passes about 35 000 test checks on the build machine. The review's fixes are in.

CheckpointWith two sensors paired, each drives its own LED, and after a power cycle both LEDs are right again without re-pairing.

4

Multiple sensors and a 48-hour test

Pair 3 or more sensors and run the device for 48 hours. Test a battery swap, a power loss, removing and re-adding a sensor, and a sensor moved out of range.

CheckpointNo stuck LEDs. Every state change shows up within a few seconds, and dropped connections recover on their own.

5

Enclosure and handover

Mount the supply in its enclosure, strain-relieve the mains lead, and tidy the low-voltage side. Write a README covering pairing, removal and reflashing. With 8 MB of flash, over-the-air updates become an option later.

6

One unit per room

Build one Glugg per room, each for about 30 windows. Same firmware and wiring, with these changes, sized from the memory each sensor used in Phase 4:

  • Slots and LEDs: about 30 slots, and a WS2812 strip of about 30 LEDs in place of the 8-LED stick, on the same GPIO13 and 74HCT125. All 30 lit amber draws about 0.2 A, so the supply stays the same. The saved slot table gets a new version and is migrated.
  • Thread: the S3's child table goes from 10 to at least 32. One room is very likely within direct radio range of its S3, so no extra Thread router is needed; check each sensor's link quality with matter esp ot_cli child table.
  • Matter: the secure session pool goes from 17 to about 40, with the exchange and handshake pools to match. Reconnects after a power cut are staggered, about 8 sensors at a time, so recovery takes a minute or two instead of seconds.
  • Pairing: pair <code> <slot>, so window 14 can be LED 14 instead of the lowest free slot.
  • Neighbours: each unit runs its own Thread network with its own keys. Two rooms may pick the same channel; they only share airtime, which is tiny at this load.

CheckpointOne room unit runs 30 sensors for 48 hours with no stuck LEDs, and recovers every sensor on its own after a power cut. Then build the other three.

5 · Findings

What the build and the review taught us

Found on 30 Sep 2026 while installing the toolchain, running the simulation, building the firmware and reviewing it, by reading the ESP-IDF, OpenThread and esp-matter sources that the build uses.

Matter points its DNS client at whichever SRP server its own SRP client finds.On the S3 that is its own lookup service, so no custom lookup configuration is needed.
OpenThread's SRP server moves to a new port every time it starts.After a power cut, sensors notice the change and re-register within seconds instead of waiting out their 2-hour lease.

6 · Decisions

Decisions (all settled)

Decided · Fully offline

No Wi-Fi. The S3 runs the Thread network and its lookup service on its own. Phase 1 proves this before anything else.

Decided · Hardware

ESP32-S3-Zero-N8R8 as the Matter controller, with the ESP32-H2-Zero as its Thread radio. The ESP32-C5-Zero was ruled out: no PSRAM, and an open bug that stops new devices joining Thread while Bluetooth is running.

Decided · LED colours and states

Amber for open, off for closed, a green pulse while pairing, and a dim blue blink when a sensor hasn't been heard from. Added later: a white sweep while starting up, dim white until a sensor's first report, and red when the Thread radio doesn't answer.

Decided · One unit per room

Four rooms with about 30 windows each get four independent Gluggs, each showing its own windows with one LED per window. One central controller for all 120 sensors was ruled out: no one is known to run esp-matter's controller at that size, it would need a Thread router in every room, and one fault would blind every room.

Decided · Pairing from the USB serial console

Connect a computer to the S3 and type pair <11-digit code>. Pairing from an iPhone is parked (below).

Chosen during the build

Defaults picked while writing the firmware. Any of them can change.

Console commands

Slots are numbered 1–8. factory-reset needs the word confirm: it asks each sensor to leave, waits until they all answer (at most 2 minutes, since sleepy sensors answer when they wake), erases the slots, the Matter fabric and the Thread network, and reboots. identify <slot> makes the sensor itself blink for 10 s; the stick doesn't change.

Timings

Blue after 2 minutes without a connection. Retries every 10 s, doubling to at most 5 minutes. Subscriptions ask for a report at least every 5 minutes (a sleepy sensor may choose longer). Pairing gives up after 5 minutes.

Certificates

The firmware trusts every production root certificate in the CSA's registry, not only IKEA's, and none of Matter's test certificates.

Parked for later

Explored but not decided. Revisit once the first MYGGBETT pairs (Phase 2), and before the room units (Phase 6).

Parked · Pairing from your iPhone

For convenience: scan a sensor's QR code instead of typing its code on a computer. The S3 would get a Bluetooth console with the same commands as the USB one, kept alongside it, and open only for a few minutes after you press its BOOT button. On the phone, start with a ready-made Bluetooth terminal app (Bluefruit Connect or nRF Toolbox), and perhaps later a small app of your own that scans the QR code and, for the room units, lets you pick the window. Glugg stays offline: no Wi-Fi and no Apple Home, and Safari can't do Bluetooth from a web page. The main risk is the S3 talking to your phone and to the sensor over Bluetooth at the same time.

Key facts from the documentation

RS-15-5

  • 85–264 VAC input, 5 V at 3 A output, adjustable 4.75–5.5 V
  • Terminals in order: L, N, FG, −V, +V, then the ADJ pot
  • Protected against overload (recovers on its own), overvoltage above 5.75–6.75 V, and overheating
  • FG must be earthed. M3 terminal screws, 6–8 kgf·cm.

Datasheet 2026-04-10 · installation manual 2025-12-17

ESP32-S3-Zero-N8R8

  • Dual-core ESP32-S3 at 240 MHz, 512 KB SRAM, 8 MB flash, 8 MB PSRAM. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth LE, no 802.15.4. Wi-Fi stays off in this design.
  • Left edge from the USB end: 5V, GND, 3V3, GPIO1–6. Right edge: TX (43), RX (44), GPIO13–7. More GPIOs are pads underneath.
  • 5V pin wired straight to USB VBUS. ME6217 regulator, 3.3 V, 800 mA max.
  • Onboard WS2812 on GPIO21, BOOT button on GPIO0, native USB on GPIO19/20
  • Pins that affect boot: GPIO0, 3, 45 and 46. GPIO33–37 are used by the PSRAM.

Electrokit 41036435 · Waveshare docs and schematic

ESP32-H2-Zero

  • ESP32-H2FH4: 802.15.4 and Bluetooth LE, 4 MB flash
  • 5V pin wired straight to USB VBUS, with no diode or fuse. ME6217 regulator (3.3 V).
  • UART0: TX on GPIO24 (with a 499 Ω series resistor) and RX on GPIO23. In this design these carry the radio link to the S3.
  • USB is the chip's own USB serial/JTAG, used to flash the RCP firmware

Waveshare schematic · ESP32-H2 datasheet v1.3

WS2812 stick

  • Input pads: GND, DIN, 4-7VDC, GND. The DOUT end is for chaining.
  • 60 mA per LED at full white, so 480 mA for all 8
  • The data line must reach at least 0.7 × supply voltage to count as high (3.5 V at 5 V), so the ESP32's 3.3 V is out of spec
  • 800 kHz, GRB order. Use a reset gap of at least 280 µs to cover all LED variants.

Worldsemi WS2812B / V5 datasheets · Adafruit NeoPixel Überguide

MYGGBETT E2492

  • Matter over Thread, paired over Bluetooth. Vendor ID 0x117C, product ID 0x8007, firmware 1.0.9–1.1.6.
  • Endpoint 1 is a Contact Sensor with Identify and BooleanState. StateValue is true when closed.
  • A sleepy Thread device with short check-in intervals. It doesn't support the Check-In protocol, so the controller must keep a subscription alive.
  • Pairing window is 15 minutes, opened by inserting the battery or pressing the button once. Factory reset: hold 10 seconds.
  • The Matter code is printed on the sensor and on the Advice & Care booklet. Don't use the yellow IKEA-app code.
